Pattern headline

Easy wins: developer tools, productivity, ecommerce, scraping/data, and most support tools. They usually expose REST or GraphQL, support API keys or OAuth, and allow a developer to get credentials without a sales motion.

Outreach required: ads, fintech data, enterprise sales/support, and AI meeting/research products. They often need app review, partner enrollment, admin approval, or paid enterprise plans.

        Where a human was needed

        Human review mattered where the docs were ambiguous about commercial access. The agent could find "API exists," but not always whether a new third-party integration can legally ship without app review, partner approval, or an enterprise contract.

        Manual corrections mainly changed verdicts from "Yes" to "Partial" for platform APIs with review gates, and from "Partial" to "No" for products whose public docs are not integration APIs.

        Hits

        Correctly identified OAuth-heavy platforms like Salesforce, HubSpot, Slack, Shopify, Google Ads, Meta, Stripe, Xero, and QuickBooks; API-key/tool-token developer products like Vercel, Datadog, Sentry, Firecrawl, Apify, DataForSEO, and SendGrid; and open-source/CLI surfaces like Twenty, Sherlock, and Mermaid CLI.

        Misses Fixed

        LinkedIn Ads, Pinterest, Amazon SP-API, Ramp, Brex, and PitchBook were initially over-scored as self-serve. Corrected to review, partner, or enterprise-gated. NotebookLM, Fathom, Consensus, Fanbasis, and Higgsfield were marked low-confidence because public app APIs were weak or unclear.
